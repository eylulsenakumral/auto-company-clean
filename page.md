# A Redditor Analyzed 9,300+ 'I Wish This Existed' Posts. Here Is What The Market Actually Wants. | Markethunt

![A Redditor Analyzed 9,300+ 'I Wish This Existed' Posts. Here Is What The Market Actually Wants.](/_next/image?url=%2Fimages%2Finsights%2Freddit-market-validation-analysis.webp&w=3840&q=75)

Founders often fall into a dangerous trap: building what they _think_ people want, rather than what people are actively asking for.

Market validation usually involves expensive surveys or awkward cold DMs. But sometimes, the market screams its needs into the void. A developer recently scraped and analyzed a massive dataset of **9,363 posts** from Reddit communities where users describe their pain points and ask for software that doesn't exist yet.

The dataset, processed by [a developer on Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/SaaS/comments/1q5lfur/i_analyzed_9300_i_wish_there_was_an_app_for_this/), covers six months of "I wish there was an app for this" requests. The results are counter-intuitive and expose a major gap between what founders are building (AI wrappers) and what users actually need.

Here are the 6 data-backed opportunities hidden in the noise.

## 1\. The "Anti-Cloud" Rebellion (7% of Requests)

While the VC world is obsessed with cloud-native SaaS, the users are moving in the opposite direction. **A significant 7% of all requests specifically asked for offline-first or privacy-focused tools.**

Users are citing "subscription fatigue" and privacy concerns. They want local-only versions of popular productivity apps. This aligns with the growing demand in the [Personal Cloud Market](/markets/personal-cloud-market) and specific segments of [Edge Security](/markets/edge-security-market).

**The Opportunity:** A "Buy Once, Use Forever" local productivity tool that syncs via iCloud/Local Network rather than a proprietary server.

> **Why This Matters:** When 1 in 14 users explicitly requests offline functionality in a world dominated by cloud services, you're looking at a meaningful counter-trend. This isn't noise - it's a rebellion against the SaaS status quo.

## 2\. The "Willingness to Pay" Paradox

The analysis separated **"Volume"** (how many people ask for it) from **"Value"** (how many people used words like _buy_, _price_, _premium_).

### The Volume Trap

**Productivity** had the most requests (1,231 posts). It's crowded, loud, and competitive.

### The Revenue Reality

**Finance** had the highest concentration of "pay signals."

Users aren't looking for another free to-do list. They're looking for specialized portfolio trackers, risk analysis tools, and banking dashboards. They explicitly mentioned wanting "premium" versions that handle data securely. The [Financial Wellness Software Market](/markets/financial-wellness-software-market) is seeing this shift firsthand as users move from simple tracking to complex planning.

**Insight:** If you want users, build Productivity. If you want revenue, build Finance.

## 3\. The "Frustration Index" (Long Rants = Good Markets)

The analysis used an interesting metric: **Post Length**. The logic is that a short post implies a mild inconvenience, while a long, detailed post implies deep, unresolved pain.

Three categories had the highest "Frustration Scores" (avg. 200+ words per post):

### Developer Platforms

Engineers writing technical essays about missing features in AWS or NetSuite. This mirrors the complexity seen in the [DevOps Market](/markets/devops-market) and [Cloud Native Application Market](/markets/cloud-native-application-market).

### Parenting

Parents describing complex needs for tracking sleep, milestones, and school schedules.

### Cooking

Users are _furious_ about "bloated" recipe sites buried under ads and SEO life stories. While the [AI in Food & Beverages Market](/markets/artificial-intelligence-ai-in-food-beverages-market) is booming for enterprise, the consumer side is broken.

**The Opportunity:** A minimalist, text-only recipe manager that strips away the "blog" aspect would solve a high-frustration problem immediately.

## 4\. The "ADHD" Super-Users

Surprisingly, the **r/ADHD** subreddit emerged as one of the highest-signal communities for software developers.

These users aren't asking for generic tools. They're providing detailed feature specifications because current workflows fail them. They're hyper-aware of UI/UX friction and represent a massive, underserved niche for highly specific workflow tools.

**Why This Is Gold:**

-   They articulate problems better than most product managers
-   They're willing to pay for tools that actually work
-   They become evangelists when something finally solves their problem
-   The ADHD population represents 4-5% of adults globally - that's a huge TAM overlapping with the [Digital Therapeutics Market](/markets/digital-therapeutics-market).

## 5\. Timing: The "Monday Blues" Spike

The data shows a clear behavioral trend: **frustration peaks on Mondays and Tuesdays.**

People start their work week, hit a wall with their current inefficient software, and go to Reddit to complain or search for alternatives.

**Actionable Tip:** If you're launching on Product Hunt or running ads, align your push with the Monday/Tuesday frustration spike. This is when people are actively looking for solutions.

## 6\. What's Heating Up Right Now (Q1 2026)

Looking at the most recent 60-day trend, two categories are spiking:

### Health & Wellness

Driven by "New Year" resolutions, people are hunting for habit-builders and gym trackers. This seasonal spike correlates perfectly with the [Fitness App Market](/markets/fitness-app-market) growth cycles.

### Smart Home Visualization

People have the hardware (sensors), but they hate the software. There's a surge in demand for better graphs and dashboards to visualize home data (temperature, energy usage) over time. Demand is shifting from hardware to data, driving the [Smart Home Energy Monitoring Devices Market](/markets/smart-home-energy-monitoring-devices-market).

**The Pattern:** Users don't need more IoT devices - they need better software to make sense of the devices they already own.

## What NOT to Build (According to the Data)

Just as important as knowing what to build is knowing what to avoid:

**✗ Another AI Wrapper** - Users want tools that solve specific problems, not "ChatGPT but for X"

**✗ Generic Productivity Apps** - The space is saturated. Unless you have a unique angle, stay away.

**✗ Social Platforms** - Almost no requests for "new social networks." Users have platform fatigue.

**✗ Crypto/Web3 Tools** - Mentioned in less than 1% of posts. The hype has died down significantly.

This analysis gives you a massive shortcut, but it's not a crystal ball. Here's how to act on these insights:

Based on your skills and interests, choose from the high-signal categories:

-   Developer Tools (technical users with clear specs)
-   Parenting (deep frustration, loyal users)
-   Cooking (low barrier to entry, high frustration)

### Step 2: Validate the Market Size

Now that you know where the pain is, go back to Reddit:

### Step 4: Build the Minimum Viable Solution

Based on the frustration scores, you know these users are _desperate_ for a solution. You don't need perfection - you need to solve the core problem quickly.

## The Meta-Lesson: Listen to User Behavior, Not User Opinions

This dataset is powerful because it captures **unsolicited** feedback. These aren't people responding to a survey - they're voluntarily expressing pain points.

**The hierarchy of signal quality:**

1.  **Best:** What people do (actual behavior)
2.  **Good:** What people complain about (this Reddit data)
3.  **Okay:** What people say they want (interviews)
4.  **Worst:** What people say they might want (surveys)

Reddit posts fall into category #2 - which is far more reliable than traditional market research.
